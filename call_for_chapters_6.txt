Call for Chapters: Digital Marketing and Brand Visibility in the Age of Generative Discovery

Introduction
Brand discovery is moving away from brand-controlled channels and ranked link lists toward synthesised answers produced by generative systems and toward algorithmically curated feeds. Under these conditions, the classical antecedents of brand performance, such as attention, salience and awareness, are determined less by the exposure a firm buys or controls, and more by whether a brand is retrieved, cited and accurately represented by a model that stands between the consumer and the market. This book examines what that displacement means for digital marketing theory and practice. Previous studies formalised generative engine optimisation and established that classical keyword tactics have a negligible effect on citation probability, whereas source credibility and quotation substantially increase it (Aggarwal et al., 2024), and the measurement literature has followed suit. Marketing scholarship, by contrast, has approached generative AI mainly as a production and research instrument; as a content generator whose output can exceed human benchmarks under specified conditions (Hartmann et al. 2025), as a strategic implementation problem (Grewal et al. 2025), as a method for consumer research (Yoo et al., 2025), and as an ethics and public policy question (Hermann & Puntoni, 2025). In parallel, advertising research has advanced the instrumentation of attention itself (Bruns et al., 2025). This book intends to provide a sustained treatment of how generative mediation reallocates brand visibility, and of how attention constructs and measures transfer to a setting where the first audience for brand content is a retrieval-and-synthesis pipeline. The book consolidates fast-moving evidence, provides vocabulary and measurement conventions for an emerging research stream, and establishes an agenda that marketing scholars can take up without first having to reconstruct the technical literature.

Objective
The book treats brand visibility as a dependent variable in machine-mediated environments and organises contributions around four questions: how attention and salience should be conceptualised when a model performs the initial filtering; how visibility in generative and recommender systems can be measured with defensible instruments; what marketing actions plausibly influence it; and what distributional and regulatory consequences follow. Chapters combine conceptual work, empirical studies, case-based analysis and sector applications.
Target Audience
The intended audience are academic researchers and doctoral students in marketing, advertising, communication, information systems and tourism; instructors of graduate courses in digital marketing, brand management and marketing analytics; brand managers, media planners, search and content strategists, and analytics practitioners; policy analysts and regulators concerned with algorithmic visibility, disclosure and market concentration; and academic librarians building collections in digital marketing and applied AI.
Recommended Topics
The following topics provide several suggestions for authors.
Conceptual foundations
• Attention as a scarce resource: revisiting attention economics for machine-mediated markets
• Brand salience and mental availability when retrieval precedes recall
• Rethinking brand awareness constructs under generative mediation
• Consideration set formation when a model performs the first filtering
• Consumer-based brand equity in the absence of direct exposure
• Model representations of brands as a form of brand image
• Theoretical limits of hierarchy-of-effects models in algorithmic discovery
Generative and algorithmic discovery
• Generative engine optimisation: evidence, mechanisms and marketing implications
• Answer engine optimisation and the decline of click-through as an outcome
• Zero-click search and the erosion of brand-owned traffic
• Entity strategy: knowledge graphs, structured data and brand disambiguation
• Recommender systems and feed curation as visibility gatekeepers
• Retrieval-augmented generation and the strategic value of citable content
• Agentic commerce and AI purchasing assistants
• Third-party sources (encyclopaedic entries, review platforms, trade press) as visibility infrastructure
Human attention
• Scalable computational measures of attention and their validation
• Eye-tracking, biometric and neuromarketing evidence on brand attention
• Short-form video and attentional allocation to brand content
• Attention currencies in media planning and trading
• Ad avoidance, banner blindness and attention decay
• Attention quality versus attention quantity in brand-building outcomes
Content, creators and intermediaries
• Influencers and creators as attention brokers
• User-generated content as an input to generative brand representations
• Authenticity, disclosure and consumer response to AI-generated brand content
• Synthetic influencers and virtual brand personas
• Community and forum content as a source of model-visible brand signals
Measurement and methods
• Prompt-based auditing of brand visibility across generative platforms
• Share-of-model, citation rate and mention frequency as brand metrics
• Cross-platform variance and the instability of visibility measures
• Attribution and effectiveness measurement when discovery is unobservable
• Experimental designs for testing content interventions on model outputs
• Reconciling attention metrics with algorithmic visibility metrics
• Reliability and replicability challenges in fast-changing platform environments
Ethics, governance and market structure
• Visibility concentration and the disadvantage of small and independent brands
• Manipulation, adversarial prompt injection and the boundaries of legitimate optimisation
• Bias in model representations of brands, categories and regions
• Disclosure obligations and transparency in AI-mediated recommendation
• Implications of the EU AI Act and the Digital Services Act for brand visibility
• Consumer welfare and informed choice when synthesis replaces comparison
• Sustainability and the resource costs of visibility competition
Contexts and applications
• Destination branding and tourism in AI-mediated trip planning
• Retail and e-commerce assortment visibility
• B2B buying journeys initiated in generative search
• SMEs, resource constraints and low-cost visibility strategies
• Hospitality: independent operators versus chains in model recommendations
• Higher education, healthcare and other high-consideration categories
• Public sector and non-profit communication in algorithmic environments
• Cross-cultural and emerging-market perspectives on AI-mediated discovery
Submission Procedure
Researchers and practitioners are invited to submit on or before October 25, 2026, a chapter proposal of 1,000 to 2,000 words clearly explaining the mission and concerns of his or her proposed chapter. Authors will be notified by November 8, 2026 about the status of their proposals and sent chapter guidelines.Full chapters of a minimum of 10,000 words (word count includes references and related readings) are expected to be submitted by January 17, 2027, and all interested authors must consult the guidelines for manuscript submissions at https://www.igi-global.com/publish/contributor-resources/before-you-write/ prior to submission. All submitted chapters will be reviewed on a double-anonymized review basis. Contributors may also be requested to serve as reviewers for this project.

Note: There are no submission or acceptance fees for manuscripts submitted to this book publication, Digital Marketing and Brand Visibility in the Age of Generative Discovery. All manuscripts are accepted based on a double-anonymized peer review editorial process.

All proposals should be submitted through the eEditorial Discovery® online submission manager.
